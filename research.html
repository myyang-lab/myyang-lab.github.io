Our Research Vision

Understanding Matter Across Scales

At the Multiscale Computational Science (MCS) Lab, we seek to understand the fundamental mechanisms governing the structure, dynamics, and reactivity of complex molecular and material systems.

Our research integrates quantum mechanical calculations, atomistic simulations, multiscale modeling, and emerging AI-driven approaches to connect microscopic interactions with macroscopic properties and functions.

By bridging fundamental understanding and predictive modeling, we aim to accelerate the discovery and design of functional materials and molecular systems for applications in energy, catalysis, and biological sciences.

Research Area 01

Battery & Energy Materials

We investigate ion transport, electrochemical reactions, and interfacial phenomena in advanced energy materials using atomistic and multiscale simulations. Our goal is to explain the molecular mechanisms that govern energy storage and conversion.

Research Topics

  • Electrolytes, ion transport, and proton conduction
  • Electrode–electrolyte interfaces and SEI formation
  • Solid-state batteries and electrochemical reaction mechanisms
  • Fuel-cell ionomers and energy conversion

Research Area 02

Nanomaterials & Catalysis

We explore atomic-scale structures, electronic properties, and reactivity of nanomaterials and catalytic systems. Electronic structure calculations and molecular simulations reveal pathways to rational materials and catalyst design.

Research Topics

  • Metal and alloy nanoclusters
  • Catalytic reaction mechanisms
  • Electrochemical CO₂ conversion
  • Nanomaterial formation, transformation, and stability

Our Research Philosophy

From Fundamental Understanding to Predictive Design

Scientific discovery begins with understanding the fundamental principles governing complex systems. We see computational science not merely as a means of interpreting experiments, but as an approach for revealing mechanisms, testing hypotheses, and guiding discoveries.

Through physics-based modeling, advanced simulations, and emerging data-driven methods, we seek to transform molecular-level insights into rational strategies for designing materials and molecular systems with desired functions.
